ASME Go-Kart Chassis Optimization & Fabrication

The Objective

Design a rigid, lightweight frame accommodating drivetrain, steering, and braking systems while meeting strict safety margins under competition regulations. The challenge lay in engineering sufficient torsional compliance to aid mechanical cornering grip without sacrificing structural integrity under high-g lateral transitions.

Engineering & Analysis (SOLIDWORKS FEA)

Conducted structural FEA in SOLIDWORKS to maximize torsional rigidity under quasi-static cornering loads while minimizing total chassis weight. Evaluated von Mises stress distributions across static baseline loading, left cornering quasi-static load cases, and right cornering quasi-static load cases to ensure stress concentrations remained well below yield across all welded junctions.

Kinematics & Stability

Strategically lengthened the wheelbase to improve high-speed directional stability and reduce snap-oversteer. Positioned mounting plates and seat placement to optimize the longitudinal and lateral center of gravity (CG), resulting in predictable weight transfer during heavy braking into hairpins.

Fabrication & Fixturing Strategy

Constructed a full-scale CNC-routed plywood jig to locate and hold raw A513 square tubing segments rigid during tacking and final weld passes. This fixturing technique completely eliminated thermal distortion across diagonal frame rails, guaranteeing true parallel steering knuckle geometry and drivetrain alignment. A plasma-cut steel floorpan was fused to act as a structural shear plate, significantly enhancing plane torsional stiffness.

Competition Results

Achieved a competitive chassis that posted lap times 6 seconds faster than competitors across a 24-second course circuit. The frame passed all pre-race technical inspections on first pass and maintained structural alignment throughout multiple heat cycles.

The Objective

Create a low-cost, functional 3-phase outrunner motor using additive manufacturing without requiring complex machined shafts or specialized assembly fixtures. The goal was to prove the feasibility of functional electro-mechanical prime movers created using accessible consumer FDM 3D printing equipment.

Design Innovations & Geometry

Implemented an integrated-shaft geometry and toolless mechanical interlocking assembly, keeping total material cost under $25. Press-fit channels were engineered with 0.15mm interference allowances for deep-groove ball bearings directly into the printed stator core.

Performance Verification & Metrics

Achieved a verified top speed of 14,000 RPM and a stall torque of 0.22 N⋅m powered at 12V through an electronic speed controller (ESC). Phase windings were manually tensioned and terminated in a wye configuration.

Rotor Mechanical Failure (Analysis Pending)

During high-RPM unloaded test runs approaching 14,000 RPM, the initial FDM-printed rotor shell suffered mechanical failure due to excessive centrifugal forces from the embedded neodymium magnets exceeding the hoop strength of the printed perimeter wall. Formal failure analysis is pending to quantify the stress state, investigate fracture surfaces, and optimize reinforcement geometry for subsequent iterations.
